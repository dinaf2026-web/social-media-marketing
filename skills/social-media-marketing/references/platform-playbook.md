# Platform Playbook

Use these as creative defaults, not immutable platform rules. Verify current technical limits, product features, monetization rules, and ad policies when those details matter.

## Instagram

Best suited to a mix of Reels, carousels, Stories, strong visual identity, community interaction, and save/share-worthy education.

- Reels: immediate visual or verbal hook; one clear idea; readable on-screen framing.
- Carousels: strong first-slide promise; progression; each slide advances the idea; final slide gives a useful next action.
- Stories: lower-polish, timely, interactive, behind-the-scenes, FAQ, launch, proof, reminders.
- Captions: complement the creative instead of transcribing it unnecessarily.

## TikTok

Favor native pacing, clear premises, personality, demonstrations, reactions, stories, useful specificity, and recognizable series.

- Start at the interesting part.
- Write for spoken delivery rather than formal copy.
- Let the visual carry information when possible.
- Use trends only when they genuinely fit the brand and message.

## YouTube Shorts

Favor a strong premise, fast progression, visual change, payoff, and a reason to watch through the end. Adapt from long-form strategically rather than cutting arbitrary excerpts.

## YouTube long-form

Treat packaging and retention as separate jobs.

Packaging:
- title creates a clear promise/tension;
- thumbnail communicates one dominant idea quickly;
- title + thumbnail work together rather than duplicate one another.

Script:
- opening validates the click;
- orient viewers quickly;
- create a logical progression of unanswered questions/payoffs;
- demonstrate and prove when possible;
- remove filler;
- end with a relevant next action or next video.

## LinkedIn

Favor credible insight, specific experience, useful frameworks, informed opinions, case-based lessons, and professional storytelling. Avoid fake vulnerability, empty thought-leadership slogans, and unsupported business claims.

## Facebook

Adapt to the actual audience and page/community context. Useful formats often include local/community posts, offers, events, demonstrations, customer education, discussion prompts, and repurposed video. Do not assume content that works on Instagram will perform identically.

## Search keywords and hashtags

Treat search wording as part of the content, not as a list added at the end.

- Choose keywords from the audience's real question, topic, problem, product category, location, or use case.
- Place the primary wording naturally in the title, spoken opening, on-screen text, caption, description, or alt text where the platform supports it.
- Use hashtags only when they improve discovery, community context, campaign organization, or location relevance.
- Avoid hashtag dumps, unrelated trending tags, and identical hashtag blocks on every post.
- Do not invent search volume, trend status, ranking difficulty, or “best hashtag” claims.
- When current trend or search data matters, retrieve it during the task and identify the source and date.

## Accessibility and asset rights

- Use accurate captions or transcripts for spoken content.
- Keep on-screen text readable and allow enough time to read it.
- Use useful alt text where the platform supports it.
- Do not assume the brand has rights to music, footage, customer content, likenesses, or third-party creative. Flag missing permission or licensing.
- Verify current sponsorship, affiliate, and paid-endorsement disclosure rules when they apply.

## Cross-platform rule

Do not mechanically paste the same asset everywhere. Preserve the core idea but adapt:

- opening hook;
- aspect/pacing;
- caption length;
- text density;
- CTA;
- cultural tone of the platform;
- discovery/search wording.
